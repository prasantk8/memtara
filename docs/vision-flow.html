What we're building, together

Prove the fact. Never hand over the file.

Right now, sharing sensitive information is all-or-nothing: a hospital gets your entire medical history to learn your blood type; a lender gets your entire bank statement to learn you clear a threshold. Memtara lets someone prove the one fact that matters — cryptographically, provably true — while everything else in their vault never leaves their device.

02 — The mechanism

How a proof actually flows

Everything above the line happens on the person's own device. Only a proof and the specific facts they chose to disclose ever cross it — not the vault, not the other categories, not even the fact that those other categories exist.

03 — Worked example

Walk through it: Maya's emergency share

A concrete pass through the diagram above, using the actual circuit we built (emergency_session) — not a hypothetical.

01

Maya's vault, on her phone

Encrypted records under three categories: health (blood type, allergies, medications), financial, identity. Every record is hashed into one number — the vault root — that stands in for all of it.

02

She pre-authorized a one-hour emergency policy

Ahead of time, Maya set a policy: if an emergency share is triggered, disclose blood type, allergies, and key medications — and nothing else — for up to one hour.

EMERGENCY_MAX_DURATION_SECONDS = 3600
03

A paramedic requests emergency access

Her phone builds the proof on the spot: the disclosed record really is in her vault root, the one-hour window hasn't expired, and she — not anyone else — signed off on this exact disclosure.

verify_merkle_inclusion(vault_root, record_leaf, ...) verify_emergency_window(current_time, creation_time, 3600) eddsa_verify(public_key, signature, policy_commitment)
04

The hospital receives exactly three facts

"Blood type O−. Allergic to penicillin. Carries an EpiPen. Proof valid until 14:32, signed and verifiable." Not her therapy notes. Not her financial records. Not even a hint those categories exist.

05

The hospital verifies in milliseconds

No call back to Maya's phone, no copy of her vault. The hospital's system checks the proof against her public key and published vault root — cryptographically certain the facts are genuine and she authorized them.

05 — Reference

Terms, plainly

No cryptography background required to design against this.

Vault

The encrypted store of someone's records, kept on their own device. Nothing in it is ever uploaded whole.

Vault root

A single number, built by hashing every record together, that uniquely represents the entire vault without revealing any of it.

Merkle proof

A short piece of math showing one specific record is genuinely part of the vault root — without showing any other record.

Zero-knowledge proof

A proof that a statement is true that reveals nothing else about the data behind it. This is the core trick that makes selective disclosure possible.

Session policy

What someone agrees to disclose — which category, which condition, for how long, and why — before a proof is ever generated.

Nonce

A one-time number stamped into a proof so it can't be replayed later to fake a fresh disclosure.
